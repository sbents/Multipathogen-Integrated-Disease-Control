A framework for guiding integrated disease control measures through multipathogen surveillance 

Samantha J. Bents, sjbents@stanford.edu

______________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________

Overview

Global health programs have traditionally focused on single diseases. There is potential for synergy through integrated intervention delivery, particularly in areas with overlapping geographic disease burden, but there is limited methodology developed to guide integration. Here, we propose a framework to translate multipathogen surveillance data into summary indices that can guide integrated program delivery to target multiple pathogens at once. We considered several measures of diversity to quantify multipathogen infection or undervaccination across three large-scale surveys: subregions in Bangladesh (90 clusters, n = 7,187) and Kenya (89 clusters, n = 9,077), and a national survey in Cambodia (100 clusters, n = 2,150). We found that strategies guided by a measure of diversity, Rao's quadratic index, consistently identified more efficient integrated strategies than other multipathogen indices across a range of intervention combinations. We then identified case studies in each setting that used immunization as the backbone for integration and found that incorporating information from co-occurring pathogens led to only modest reductions in efficiency for vaccine-preventable diseases, while affording potentially large gains for other pathogens. Compared with a strategy that prioritized a single vaccine-preventable disease, prioritization using a multipathogen index required targeting 4% (95% CI: 0-12%) fewer spatial clusters in Bangladesh to target four additional pathogens, 10% fewer clusters in Kenya (95% CI: 3-17%), and 11% (95% CI: 5-22%) fewer clusters in Cambodia to reach 80% of all potential intervention beneficiaries across diseases. In all settings, we observed geographic clustering of multiple pathogens, with moderate to high spatial autocorrelation in Bangladesh (Moran's I = 0.58), Kenya (I = 0.56), and Cambodia (I = 0.26), indicating potential for more efficient, spatially concentrated integrated interventions. We also found that a multipathogen index was significantly associated with a multidimensional poverty index in Bangladesh (Spearman's ρ = 0.25), Kenya (ρ = 0.51), and Cambodia (ρ = 0.59), suggesting that multipathogen-guided strategies may effectively target impoverished populations with the highest public health need. The framework provides a systematic approach to guide integrated program delivery, offering potential for greater efficiency in disease control.
